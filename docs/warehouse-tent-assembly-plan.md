# Warehouse Tent Assembly Plan

Date: July 3, 2026

## Purpose

Plan the outdoor layout and factory-style assembly workflow for eight
20 ft x 20 ft party tents at a warehouse site.

The source instruction sheet is for the `OTL10026` 6 m x 6 m PE party tent.
The instructions require flat ground, low-wind conditions, and secure
anchoring before a tent is left standing.

## Planning Assumptions

- Eight tent kits are available onsite.
- Each tent footprint is approximately 20 ft x 20 ft.
- The installation area is outdoors beside or near the warehouse.
- The crew size may be one, two, or three workers.
- The surface type and anchoring method must be confirmed before raising tents.
- Tents are temporary shelters, not permanent structures.

## Recommended Layout

Use a two-row by four-column grid.

Minimum practical work zone:

```text
105 ft wide x 55 ft deep
```

This allows:

- 20 ft x 20 ft per tent
- 5 ft between tents
- 5 ft outside working clearance
- a 5 ft service aisle between the two rows

```text
Warehouse / access side

[T1] 5 ft [T2] 5 ft [T3] 5 ft [T4]

          5 ft service aisle

[T5] 5 ft [T6] 5 ft [T7] 5 ft [T8]
```

If the site is tighter, keep the same two-row pattern but do not reduce
clearance below what the crew needs to safely carry poles, unfold roof covers,
and anchor corners.

## Orientation

- Keep all zippered endwalls facing the same operational direction unless the
  warehouse loading pattern requires otherwise.
- Prefer zipper openings toward the warehouse or the main access path.
- Leave enough space for carts, forklifts, pallets, or pedestrian traffic.
- Do not place tent legs where they block dock doors, drains, fire lanes,
  emergency exits, or vehicle turning paths.

## Kit Staging

Create eight labeled staging piles before assembly:

```text
T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, T6, T7, T8
```

Each tent pile should include:

- 1 roof cover
- 8 legs
- roof pipes and horizontal connection pipes
- roof reinforcement pipes
- connectors
- sidewalls and endwalls
- bungees
- hooks
- pegs, ropes, and required ballast or anchors

For all eight tents combined, expect approximately:

Count parts before raising any frames. Missing anchors or bungees should be
resolved before assembly continues.

## Factory-Style Build Sequence

Do the same stage across all tents before moving to the next stage. This keeps
tools, parts, and worker rhythm consistent.

1. Mark all footprints.
   Mark the four corners, door direction, and center aisle before unpacking
   frames.

2. Stage all kits.
   Place one complete kit at each footprint. Keep bungees and small hardware in
   separate containers so they are not lost in grass or gravel.

3. Build all roof frames flat.
   Assemble the roof structure for T1 through T8 while each frame is on the
   ground. Install finger screws snugly, but do not force them.

4. Add roof reinforcement triangles.
   Install the reinforcement pipes on all eight roof frames before any tent is
   raised.

5. Pull roof covers while frames are low.
   Unfold roof covers over the frames. Use a clean tarp or plastic sheet if the
   ground is dirty. Attach long bungees loosely, alternating sides to balance
   tension.

6. Raise one long side.
   Install legs along one long side of each tent. With two or three workers,
   raise and support the side together. With one worker, do not raise tents in
   wind.

7. Raise the second long side.
   Install the remaining legs, level the frame, and tighten all connections.

8. Anchor immediately.
   Anchor each tent as soon as it is standing. Do not raise multiple tents and
   leave them unsecured.

9. Attach walls.
   Install sidewalls and zippered endwalls after the frame and roof are stable.
   Keep seams and hems facing inside.

10. Final inspection.
    Check screws, bungees, wall tension, zipper operation, leg seating,
    anchors, ropes, trip hazards, and clearance around warehouse operations.

## Crew Roles

### Three Workers

Best crew size for a one-day install.

- Worker 1: lead, layout, parts verification, quality check, anchor check
- Worker 2: frame assembly, roof cover handling, leg raising
- Worker 3: frame assembly, roof cover handling, wall and anchor support

The lead should stay ahead of the builders by staging the next tent and
checking completed work instead of becoming the bottleneck on one frame.

### Two Workers

Practical crew size if the site is organized and weather is calm.

- Worker 1: layout, part staging, frame assembly
- Worker 2: frame assembly, bungees, walls, anchors

Both workers should handle roof covers and raising together.

### One Worker

Use one worker for layout, staging, and flat roof-frame assembly only.

One worker should not be expected to raise and secure all tents efficiently or
safely, especially outdoors. If one worker starts the job, schedule a second
person for the raising and anchoring stage.

## Anchoring Plan

Confirm the surface before scheduling installation.

For grass or dirt:

- Use thick pegs at legs.
- Use spiral pegs and ropes at the four corners.
- Use thin pegs to secure walls.
- Check that ground is firm enough to hold pegs.

For concrete or asphalt:

- Do not rely on the included spike anchors.
- Use sandbags, water barrels, or bolt-down anchors.
- Anchor every leg and corner before leaving the tent standing.

The instruction sheet states that the included spike anchor is temporary and
not intended for permanent use.

## Weather Gate

Do not raise tents in meaningful wind.

Stop work or delay raising if:

- wind is strong enough to move the roof cover while workers are holding it
- gusts make the frame unstable
- rain makes the surface slippery
- anchors cannot be installed immediately

If wind increases after some tents are standing, stop raising new tents and
inspect or reinforce the tents already up.

## Site Readiness Checklist

- Surface type confirmed
- Exact work zone measured
- Warehouse doors, traffic lanes, drains, exits, and fire lanes clear
- Tent orientation confirmed
- All eight kits onsite
- Parts counted
- Anchoring method onsite
- Crew size confirmed
- Weather window checked
- Basic tools available
- Trash bags or bins available for packaging

## Completion Checklist

- All eight tents are standing in the planned grid
- All legs are seated and tightened
- Roof covers are evenly tensioned
- Sidewalls and endwalls are attached with seams facing inside
- Zippers open and close correctly
- Every tent is anchored
- Ropes and pegs do not create avoidable trip hazards
- Warehouse access remains clear
- Packaging and loose hardware are removed from the work area
