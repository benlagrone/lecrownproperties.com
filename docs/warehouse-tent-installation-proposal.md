# Proposal for Outdoor Warehouse Tent Installation

Date: July 5, 2026

Prepared by: LeCrown Properties

## Proposal Summary

LeCrown Properties proposes to lay out and assemble eight temporary outdoor
party tents at the warehouse site using a controlled production-line workflow.
Each tent is approximately 20 ft x 20 ft. The recommended layout is a two-row
by four-column grid with consistent door orientation, a service aisle between
rows, and enough working clearance for safe staging, frame assembly, roof-cover
handling, anchoring, and final inspection.

The recommended labor plan is a three-worker crew with one active lead/floater
and two dedicated frame, roof, wall, and anchor workers. This setup is expected
to complete the work in approximately 6 to 7.5 onsite hours if the site is
ready, all tent kits are complete, the weather is calm, and the anchoring method
is confirmed before work begins.

## Scope of Work

The proposed work includes:

- Confirm the tent work zone, access path, and safe warehouse clearances.
- Mark the eight tent footprints in a two-row by four-column layout.
- Confirm tent opening direction and keep zipper orientation consistent.
- Count and stage parts for all eight tents before frame raising begins.
- Pre-sort bungees, pegs, hooks, ropes, anchors, and wall hardware.
- Assemble frames, reinforcement pipes, legs, covers, walls, and endwalls.
- Raise and anchor each tent before moving to the next unsupported lift.
- Check screws, anchors, zippers, wall attachment, ropes, and trip hazards.
- Remove loose packaging and leave the work area orderly at completion.

The work is for temporary tent installation only. The tents should not be
treated as permanent structures or as engineered weather-rated buildings.

## Recommended Layout

The recommended plan is a 2 x 4 tent grid:

```text
Warehouse / access side

[T1] 5 ft [T2] 5 ft [T3] 5 ft [T4]

          5 ft service aisle

[T5] 5 ft [T6] 5 ft [T7] 5 ft [T8]
```

Minimum practical work zone:

```text
105 ft wide x 55 ft deep
```

This allows each 20 ft x 20 ft tent to have working clearance around the
outside and a service aisle between rows. Final placement should avoid dock
doors, drains, fire lanes, emergency exits, vehicle turning paths, pedestrian
routes, and forklift lanes.

## Recommended Crew Plan

LeCrown recommends the three-worker active production-line plan.

Worker 1 should not act as a supervisor-only role. After marking layout and
staging parts, Worker 1 should stay one step ahead of the crew and then join
the current bottleneck. Worker 1 should:

- mark the layout
- count parts
- stage the next tent
- pre-sort bungees, pegs, hooks, and anchors
- check screws, anchors, zipper orientation, and trip hazards
- help with frame assembly, roof covers, lifting, anchoring, and wall work
- correct issues whenever staging is already ahead

Worker 2 should lead frame and roof assembly, including roof pipes, connectors,
reinforcement triangles, roof-cover handling, and one side of the lift.

Worker 3 should mirror frame assembly, prepare legs and anchors, handle the
opposite side of roof covers, begin wall installation on anchored tents, and
assist with final corrections.

## Time Estimate

These estimates assume all eight kits are onsite, parts are complete, the site
is clear, weather is calm, and the correct anchoring method is available before
work begins.

| Crew Size | Operating Model | Clock Time | Labor-Hours | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 3 workers | Active specialized production line | 6 to 7.5 hr | 18 to 22.5 | Recommended |
| 2 workers | Paired build cell | 9 to 12 hr | 18 to 24 | Practical fallback |
| 1 worker | Solo one-tent-at-a-time finish | 24 to 32 hr | 24 to 32 | Not recommended |

The three-worker estimate depends on Worker 1 actively relieving bottlenecks.
If Worker 1 only checks work and prepares the next tent without joining build,
lift, anchor, or wall tasks, the estimated onsite time increases to about 7.5
to 9.5 hours.

## Proposed Workday Sequence

1. Confirm weather, surface, anchoring method, and work-zone clearances.
2. Mark the full 2 x 4 grid and door orientation.
3. Sort parts into eight labeled tent staging piles.
4. Build roof frames and reinforcement sections.
5. Pull roof covers and attach balanced bungees.
6. Raise one tent at a time.
7. Anchor each tent immediately after raising.
8. Attach walls and zippered endwalls.
9. Complete final inspection, cleanup, and loose-hardware removal.

## Site Requirements Before Scheduling

The client should confirm or provide the following before installation day:

- Clear, level work area large enough for the tent layout.
- Surface type confirmed as grass, dirt, concrete, asphalt, or other.
- Correct anchoring method onsite before tent raising begins.
- All eight tent kits delivered to the work area.
- Access to the work zone for unloading, staging, and trash removal.
- Confirm no blocked exits, fire lanes, docks, drains, or forklift lanes.
- Calm weather window for roof-cover handling and frame raising.

If the tents will be installed on concrete or asphalt, the included spike
anchors are not enough by themselves. The client should provide or approve
sandbags, water barrels, bolt-down anchors, or another appropriate ballast
method before work begins.

## Exclusions and Change Items

Unless added in writing, this proposal excludes:

- Purchase, delivery, or replacement of tent kits.
- Permit filing, engineering review, or permanent-structure approval.
- Electrical work, lighting, HVAC, heaters, generators, flooring, or signage.
- Concrete drilling or bolt-down anchors unless approved.
- Ballast rental, sandbags, or water barrels unless approved.
- Ground repair, grading, drainage correction, striping, or barricades.
- Traffic control.
- Work during unsafe wind, rain, lightning, or unstable surface conditions.
- Repairs for missing, damaged, mismatched, or defective tent parts.
- Rework caused by layout changes after assembly begins.

## Acceptance Criteria

The installation should be considered complete when:

- All eight tents are standing in the approved layout.
- Every leg is seated and tightened.
- Roof covers are evenly tensioned.
- Sidewalls and endwalls are attached with correct zipper orientation.
- Each tent is anchored using the approved anchoring method.
- Ropes, pegs, and anchors are checked for avoidable trip hazards.
- Warehouse access, emergency access, drains, and traffic paths remain clear.
- Loose packaging and unused parts are consolidated or removed.
